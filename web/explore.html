    01 / Observation

    Observe the canopy

    L-band radar measures backscatter: microwave energy returned from the landscape. HH and HV describe different transmit/receive polarizations.

    02 / Detection

    Identify a change

    An HH rise can indicate an early-stage disturbance; an HV drop can indicate a late stage. Rain, fire and flooding can also change the signal.

    03 / Validation

    Check the evidence

    Compare patches with GLAD-L reference alerts and inspect the time series. Potential radar disturbance is distinct from confirmed deforestation.

    Reading a patch's time series

    Selecting a patch opens it on the radar map, zoomed to its outline, with its HH and HV series in the controls panel.

    • HH solid line: the selected spot or patch.
    • HV solid line: clearing usually shows as HV falling away from forest.
    • Dashed lines: stable radar forest, for scale.

    Display-derived pixel values can clip at the stretch limits; patch series use the full-precision stack.

    Limitations

    • Dry season only (June to October 2026). The wet season, when cloud is heaviest, was not tested.
    • Flags compare the last two passes with the first three, so many are likely seasonal drying rather than clearing.
    • NISAR L2 GCOV is a provisional product; display layers are compressed.
    • Hansen and GLAD are optical reference products, not ground truth.
    • Selective logging, fire and flooding are not handled.
    • The first attempt, a 1 dB change detector, failed (details above and in the analysis section).

    We do not claim this stops deforestation, saves carbon, or works everywhere.

    Where it could go next

    Wet-season and multi-year passes, a seasonal correction, a method that also watches already-cleared pasture, and independent human labels.
