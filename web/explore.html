    01 / Observation

    Observe the canopy

    L-band radar measures backscatter: microwave energy returned from the landscape. HH and HV describe different transmit/receive polarizations.

    02 / Detection

    Identify a change

    An HH rise can indicate an early-stage disturbance; an HV drop can indicate a late stage. Rain, fire and flooding can also change the signal.

    03 / Validation

    Check the evidence

    Compare patches with GLAD-L reference alerts and inspect the time series. Potential radar disturbance is distinct from confirmed deforestation.

    Selecting a patch opens it on the radar map, zoomed to its outline, with its HH and HV series in the controls panel.

    05 / Validate

    Validation and limitations

    A transparent prototype for investigating forest change. Alert agreement is a comparison with another product, not independently verified accuracy.

    Held-out reference agreement

    Pending

    The optical chip check was performed by the same AI assistant that ran detection, not an independent human reviewer. Human re-labeling is needed.

    What this prototype demonstrates

    Real radar acquisitions, aligned temporal exploration, inspectable detection patches, and explicit reference comparisons in one open workflow.

    What remains uncertain

    • Rain, soil moisture, fire and flooded forest can mimic disturbances.
    • Selective logging is not detected. Detection was tested on one study area.
    • Provisional radar products and compressed display layers introduce limitations.
    • Low reference agreement does not establish new forest clearing. Independent verification remains essential.

    Where it could go next

    Human-reviewed labels, improved noise rejection, and further study areas could support a better-tested monitoring workflow.
