Novo Progresso, Pará · BR-163

The NextFrontier.

For much of the rainy season, optical satellites can't see the Amazon through the clouds. NISAR's L-band radar can. The image behind this text is its HV image: forest shows up bright and cleared land dark. We tested what it catches and what it misses, and we report both.

    How the evidence categories are assigned

    Class-switch patches marked alert_supported keep the label they already had: they overlap a GLAD-L or GLAD-S2 alert inside the June 26–November 5 window we fixed in advance. Every other patch stays at insufficient evidence for confirmation. We don't read anything into the optical images from the regional clear fraction. We don't call a date wet or dry without an independent climatology, and we have none. There is no calibrated confidence probability.

    The environmental context only covers the 60 km southern Pará box, so full-frame patches outside it have none. This processing is kept apart from the detection and validation rules, which are frozen.

    01 / Observation

    Observe the canopy

    L-band radar measures backscatter, the microwave energy that bounces back off the ground and trees. HH and HV are two ways of sending and receiving that signal (two polarizations).

    02 / Detection

    Identify a change

    HH going up can mean a disturbance has just started. HV going down can mean it's further along. But rain, fire and flooding change the signal too.

    03 / Validation

    Check the evidence

    Check patches against GLAD-L alerts and look at the time series. A possible disturbance on radar is not the same thing as confirmed deforestation.

    Limitations

    • We only have the dry season (June to October 2026). We didn't test the wet season, which is when cloud is worst.
    • Flags compare the last two passes with the first three, so many are likely seasonal drying rather than clearing.
    • NISAR L2 GCOV is still a provisional product, and the display layers are compressed.
    • Hansen and GLAD come from optical satellites. We use them as a reference, but they aren't ground truth.
    • We don't handle selective logging, fire or flooding.
    • Our first try, a 1 dB change detector, failed. Details are above and in the analysis section.

    We do not claim this stops deforestation, saves carbon, or works everywhere.

    Where it could go next

    Passes from the wet season and from more than one year. A correction for the seasons. A method that also watches pasture that's already been cleared. And labels from people who weren't part of the project.
