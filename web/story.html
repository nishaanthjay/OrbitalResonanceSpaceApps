Can a satellite see the Amazon being cleared through the clouds?

Optical satellites lose this forest under cloud for much of the year. Radar doesn't. We tested NISAR, the new NASA–ISRO radar satellite, on 20 km of forest frontier near Novo Progresso, Pará, across nine passes from June to October 2026.

Short answer: it maps forest well. It does not yet catch new clearing. Here is what we found, and why.

Watch how the radar signal changes from pass to pass, compared with all earlier passes. Clearing should show up as the forest signal dropping.

signal droppedsignal rose

Some drops are real change. Many are noise, rain and drying soil: radar responds to moisture too. Telling those apart is the hard part.

Does it catch clearing? Not yet

We checked our flagged patches against GLAD, the optical alert system, and against Sentinel-2 photos.

Our flags: ground signal roseOur flags: forest signal droppedGLAD alerts 2026
  • 92.5%of 2026 GLAD alerts here fall on land that was already cleared before 2026: pasture being re-cleared. Our method only watches standing forest, so it cannot see these by design.
  • 2 of 57of our flags in the held-back half matched a GLAD alert.
  • 0 of 20flags we could check in Sentinel-2 photos showed visible clearing.

So radar and the optical alerts are watching different land, and at a 1 dB threshold our flags are mostly noise. That is a real result, and it tells us what to fix.

Photo check: 45 points, labelled blind to which were flags. 9 of 15 held-back flags had no clear photo. Labelled by our AI assistant, not yet by an independent person. Full numbers: validation/results_final.md.

6 of 6

The next frontier

NISAR passes over every 12 days, and its record is only months old. Three changes could turn this map into an early warning:

  • More passesRequire a drop to persist over several passes, so one wet day doesn't trigger a flag.
  • A pasture-aware methodMost clearing here is re-clearing of old pasture, which a forest-only method misses.
  • Independent checksField or high-resolution labels, so accuracy is measured rather than inferred.

We don't claim this stops deforestation or works everywhere. We tested one 20 km area with a provisional data product.
